Why generic platforms fall short.

The gaps that show up
after you go live.

Banks and fintechs discover that off-the-shelf payment infrastructure doesn't account for the operational complexity of regulated financial businesses.

01

A payment doesn't end when it's submitted

It needs routing, state tracking, reversal handling, settlement, and a full dispute trail. Most integrations handle the happy path. The edge cases are what break operations at 2am.

02

Your approval flows aren't what the platform assumed

Banks have maker-checker requirements, internal signing hierarchies, and controls that no off-the-shelf product was built for. You end up working around the tool rather than with it.

03

Five banks, three providers, two ledgers — each behaves differently

Every integration has its own protocol, timeout behaviour, and failure mode. Without a proper orchestration layer, that inconsistency spreads through your product and your operations team.

04

Audit trails aren't something you can retrofit

When regulators ask for transaction records or a reconciliation breaks at month-end, the answer depends on decisions made at design time — not something you can bolt on later.
